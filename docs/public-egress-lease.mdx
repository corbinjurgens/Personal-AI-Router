# Public egress lease

Public dispatch is disabled. The single build owner is
`remoteDispatchAvailable=false` in
[`shared/nodepolicy/routing.go`](../services/shared/nodepolicy/routing.go).
Policy validation uses it directly; the read-only `PublicDispatchAvailable()`
accessor supplies G's early and final production gates. Disabled provider
configuration may be staged, but enabled deployments and remote/paid tier
permissions cannot be saved. There is no environment variable, CLI flag, mutable
test switch, policy field or alternate enable bit. Production public traffic
remains zero.

G supplies a broker-issued, proxy-enforced lease and one guarded attempt wrapper.
It does not expose a public HTTP route or renderer capability. The wrapper is
still unwired from production ingress. A successful private lease acknowledgement,
model listing, account status or synthetic test guard cannot authorize disclosure.
The [proxy specification](../services/nvpair-proxy/spec.md#129-public-egress-lease-and-stable-consumer-g-disabled)
is the normative component contract; this page traces its owners and acceptance
limits.

Lease state is neither durable policy nor a second accounting owner. Public
permission is accepted separately for every attempt. A stream accepted before
revocation is in flight and may finish with its accounting retained; revocation
prevents the next acceptance. The mutex boundary does not make a socket write
physically atomic.

## Receiver-clock protocol

Only the trusted broker parent calls these process-scoped proxy methods. Public
broker clients receive method-not-found; generic client/peer relays do not forward
them. Neither method is an engine-manager route or a desktop bridge capability.

| Method | Request | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `node/renew-egress-lease` | `{runId, revision, sequence, notAfterNanos}` | `{runId, revision, sequence, nowNanos}` |
| `node/revoke-egress-lease` | `{runId, sequence}` | `{runId, sequence}` |

`runId` identifies the exact proxy process; `revision` must equal its active
policy revision. Sequence is a canonical positive decimal string within signed
64-bit range. Deadlines and clock samples are canonical nonnegative decimal
strings in the receiver's monotonic clock domain, never JSON numbers or
wall-clock timestamps. Strict decoding rejects unknown fields and malformed
values; binding, sequence and deadline refusals return JSON-RPC `-32602` without
changing authority.

A renewal with `notAfterNanos: "0"` is anchor-only: it validates the ready active
run/revision, advances the sequence high-water and samples `nowNanos` without
changing an existing deadline. It does not grant or withdraw permission.

For a valid anchor reply the broker records returned proxy elapsed time `p0` at
broker monotonic receipt time `b0`. At a later eligible issuance/request-start
time `b`, after acquiring the push slot and before codec I/O, it calculates:

```text
notAfter = p0 + (b - b0) + 5 seconds
```

The proxy sampled `p0` before the broker received it at `b0`. The processes share
the host monotonic time rate, so this conservative elapsed calculation expires
no later than issuance plus five seconds. Pipe delay, a synchronous handler and
late acknowledgements consume validity instead of restarting it. A severely
delayed anchor can shorten or deny a grant. Negative elapsed time or signed
overflow denies issuance.

The anchor belongs to the proxy run. Expiry or age alone does not re-anchor it;
run replacement, explicit withdrawal or rejected reply binding invalidates it.
A transient renewal transport/RPC error leaves an existing anchor intact.

The receiver accepts a nonzero deadline only when both `now < notAfter` and
`notAfter - now <= 5 seconds`. Permission refuses at exact equality
`now >= notAfter`, with no grace. Renewal also requires the current run/revision,
completed account activation, no activation transition and a sequence strictly
above the run's high-water.

Renewals and revokes share that high-water across policy changes and withdrawal.
A higher-sequence revoke for the current run clears the deadline and lease
revision without activating policy. Duplicate or reordered messages cannot
restore revoked authority. Sequence exhaustion never wraps. A replacement starts
with a new run and empty lease state; old-run bytes cannot grant it authority.

## Broker renewal and withdrawal

One context-bound renewal job belongs to the broker's `Serve` lifetime. It starts
without a grant and re-evaluates eligibility once per second. Every attempt
requires, under `policyRuntime.mu`:

- Loaded policy (`ready`) and no `recoveryRequired`.
- The current live proxy equal to both `incarnation` and `egress.process`, with
  a nonempty acknowledged `runID` and an unclosed process lifetime.
- `activeRevision == current.Revision`, no activation or availability error,
  no pending policy/availability push and no withdrawal latch.

The existing policy push slot serializes lease calls with policy/availability
pushes. A one-second context covers slot acquisition and the RPC. The broker
rechecks the same process/run/revision and eligibility after acquiring the slot,
then validates the reply and current process again on return. It never holds the
runtime mutex across codec I/O. Missed ticks do not accumulate retry jobs.
Already constructed bytes keep their original run binding.

Adopting a new desired revision stops renewal of the old one. An unacknowledged
policy/availability push, failed activation, lost/unreadable acknowledgement,
wrong origin/run/revision, recovery, process exit/replacement or withdrawal
cannot supply eligibility. Ordinary renewal transport errors and RPC refusals
skip the tick and are re-evaluated on the next tick; they do not require a new
policy edit. A decoded renewal reply with an invalid run/revision/sequence
binding invalidates the anchor and records both policy and availability errors.
Fresh exact acknowledgement of both is required before re-anchoring/granting.

`persistPolicy` adopts the complete new document on `durablefile.ErrReplaced`
while preserving the save error: only directory-sync durability is uncertain.
Its exactly acknowledged active revision remains eligible; G adds no separate
durability tracker. A pre-replacement failure retains the old desired document.
Invalid input and stale CAS cannot manufacture a persisted revocation.

Credential-reference replacement and intentional shutdown first latch withdrawal,
then attempt a higher-sequence revoke on the exact old process within one second.
They stop/join that proxy even if slot acquisition, transport or acknowledgement
fails. `Serve` cancels/joins the renewal job before proxy teardown. A crash makes
the old process ineligible; replacement policy/availability replay establishes
activation, followed by a new anchor, never an inherited lease. Proxy broker-stdin
EOF cancels its request context; it supplies no engine-idle guarantee.

Policy results and `policy:changed` retain persisted desired revision,
`activeRevision`, `activation` (`pending`, `error`, `active`) and operational error
text. A failed restrictive push never reports that new revision enforced; no new
public attempt can be accepted after the last grant's issuance-plus-five-second
bound. Policy activation status is not public permission. G3's scheduled
binding-error publication is bounded to one sender plus one waiter. Its underlying
parent client-write lifetime remains a separate unresolved transport limit;
bounded publication count does not promise bounded fanout completion. Existing
departed-run workload retirement, accepted-tail handling and capped shutdown join
retain their own contract.

## Stable consumer contract for I

I may call `publicAttemptPermitted(tier, snapshot) error` to skip a public
candidate before body construction or qualification. Success is provisional and
cannot be cached as a grant. Each production attempt must use this existing
wrapper signature:

```go
func (p *Proxy) runPublicChatAttempt(ctx context.Context, tier string,
    snapshot chatAccountSnapshot, body []byte, q *chatQualification,
    transport *publicClient, resolve func(nodepolicy.CredentialRef) (string, bool),
    budget chatByteBudget, lifecycle *publicAttemptLifecycle,
    emit func([]byte) error) (chatParseResult, error)
```

I supplies its trusted local-origin tier/mode decision, immutable D snapshot and
E qualification, selected transport/reference resolver, one request context,
finite process-budget/emit hooks and an attempt-bound lifecycle. A trial account
does not qualify as permanently free. G requires `PaidAllowed` for `BillingPaid`;
I retains classification, ordering and free-only eligibility.

G's early wrapper observes the current validated account, even for a refused
candidate, to tighten the request's allowance; the pinned snapshot cannot supply
an obsolete ceiling. It checks build availability, exact active policy/lease,
matching enabled deployment/account and tier API membership, remote/family and
paid permissions. D/E then prepare qualified bounded content, resolve only the
selected canonical credential reference, and durably reserve against the remaining
request allowance. D rechecks the whole snapshot under its ledger mutex.

The E runner's injected interface stays unchanged:

```go
type publicAttemptGuard func(context.Context, func() error) error
```

It first calls the guard with a nil continuation after reserve. At the final
handoff E supplies `lifecycle.acceptedByGuard` as the continuation. G's
`publicPermissionAccept` holds the lease mutex, then admission, then enters I's
request transition mutex. This rechecks policy/lease and accepts only the current,
uncancelled, unexpired attempt with a confirmed durable hold. The caller never
enters G while holding the request mutex.

E completes request construction, finite headroom admission, credential resolution
and DNS validation/pinning before final acceptance. The callback is immediately
before `http.Client.Do`, with no blocking work between successful acceptance and
`Do`; permission locks are released before I/O. Policy activation and lease revoke
serialize with acceptance, not with filesystem sync, credential lookup, HTTP I/O
or streaming. Existing accepted work retains D/E accounting.

| Typed wrapper refusal | Liability and send state |
| --- | --- |
| `publicBeforeReserve` / `"before-reserve"` | No new hold; invalid lifecycle or early permission refusal. |
| `publicAfterReserve` / `"after-reserve"` | Advisory post-reserve permission refusal; hold retained, no send. |
| `publicFinalGuard` / `"final-guard"` | Final refusal immediately before send; proven unsent, hold retained. |

`publicAttemptRefusal` unwraps to `errPublicPermission`. Reasons distinguish
`permission-refused`, `cancelled-before-send` and `request-lifecycle-refused`
where applicable. E preserves `errPublicFinalGuard` for the wrapper instead of
collapsing it into `errChatAttempt`; G maps it to the typed final refusal. This
unsent branch performs no provider-outcome, money or qualification completion.
Other D/E errors retain their categories: reservation/accounting failure can
leave a durable or uncertain liability and is not proof of no hold. There is no
G-owned refund or release operation.

`routingSpend` remains I's one logical request owner across destinations and
regeneration. It retains `RoutingRequestKey` (`OriginNodeID`, `ProxyRunID`,
`RequestID`), total attempts, one pre-output deadline, cancellation and cumulative
paid allowance. `associated` records a durable/uncertain D reservation even if
cancellation won during the write. Settlement never subtracts granted bounds or
refills the allowance. Unknown durability stops further spending.

`firstSuccessfulByte` commits through the existing request owner at the first
positive raw successful-response read, including bytes later rejected by framing
or parsing; discarded provider error bytes do not commit. The owned `responseSink`
supplies delivery facts and replaces the standalone synthetic `emit` seam. I owns
the aggregate inference byte root, output commitment, cancellation/regeneration
and terminal delivery; G's run binding is not a replacement request identity.
I must not call the raw runner or `post`/`postObserved` in production, add a second
reserve, retry inside the guard, cache guard success or treat a wire ACK as
permission. Public ingress integration remains pending.
